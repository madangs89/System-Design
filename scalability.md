Horizontal and Vertical Scaling | System Design

Scaling is the process of increasing a system's capacity to handle growing workloads.
Scalability is simply measured by the number of requests an application can handle successfully.
Horizontal and vertical scaling are two common methods of expanding system resources.Horizontal scaling and Vertical scaling both involve adding resources to your computing infrastructure.

Scaling can be performed manually or automatically based on demand.
The choice of scaling method depends on workload, architecture and resource requirements.


Vertical Scaling
Vertical scaling, also known as scaling up, involves increasing the resources of an existing machine, such as CPU, RAM or storage, without adding more machines.
Vertical scaling is basically makes the nodes stronger. If you have 1 server, make the server stronger with adding more hardware.

Horizontal Scaling
Horizontal scaling, also known as scaling out, involves adding more machines or application instances to distribute workloads. A load balancer is commonly used to distribute incoming requests across instances.
